# Launch into Computing – Unit 8 Reflection
## Principles of Cyber Security

---

## What? (Description)

In Unit 8, I explored the principles of cybersecurity and their importance in protecting modern enterprise systems, networks, and data from malicious attacks and unauthorised access. The unit introduced key cybersecurity concepts such as common cyber threats and attack vectors, including phishing, ransomware, denial‑of‑service attacks, man‑in‑the‑middle attacks, and zero‑day exploits. Through the lecturecast and reading materials, the unit examined how these threats exploit both technical vulnerabilities and human behaviour, highlighting the increasing sophistication of cyberattacks in enterprise environments.

The unit also covered a range of cybersecurity tools and defence mechanisms, including firewalls, encryption, antivirus and endpoint protection, intrusion detection systems (IDS), security information and event management (SIEM) platforms, and multi‑factor authentication (MFA). In addition, the unit introduced enterprise IT risk management, disaster recovery (DR), and business continuity planning (BCP), emphasising how organisations prepare for, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity incidents. Governance and compliance frameworks such as GDPR, ISO/IEC 27001, and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework were discussed alongside ethical and professional issues, including user privacy, surveillance, ethical hacking, and the use of AI in cybersecurity. As part of the unit’s practical work, I completed a Cybersecurity Threat Assessment and Mitigation Plan, applying theoretical concepts to a realistic enterprise scenario.

---

## So what? (Interpretation)

This unit reinforced the importance of cybersecurity as a foundational concern in modern computing, particularly for enterprise systems that handle sensitive financial and customer data. Understanding the range of cyber threats and their real‑world impact demonstrated that cybersecurity is not solely a technical issue, but a combination of technology, governance, and human decision‑making. The coverage of risk management frameworks highlighted how organisations must adopt a proactive and structured approach to identifying assets, assessing vulnerabilities, and implementing layered security controls.

From a professional perspective, the unit strongly connects to my role as a Senior Application Programmer working with enterprise automation, cloud platforms, and business‑critical workflows. Many of the threats discussed, such as phishing, credential compromise, ransomware, and cloud misconfiguration, are directly relevant to environments using automation tools, APIs, and low‑code platforms. The real‑world case studies illustrated how cybersecurity failures can result in severe operational disruption, regulatory penalties, and reputational damage. The threat assessment activity was particularly valuable, as it strengthened my ability to think systematically about risks, vulnerabilities, mitigation strategies, and ethical responsibilities within enterprise systems.

---

## What next? (Action)

Going forward, I will apply the cybersecurity principles learned in this unit when designing and maintaining enterprise applications and automation solutions. This includes prioritising secure authentication mechanisms such as MFA, implementing least‑privilege access controls, ensuring encryption of sensitive data, and supporting audit logging and continuous monitoring for security visibility. I also plan to further explore zero‑trust security models and AI‑driven security tools, which are becoming increasingly important in cloud‑based and distributed environments.

Academically, this unit provides a strong foundation for subsequent units focused on ethics, security, and governance in computing. I will continue to use reflective practice in my e‑portfolio to connect cybersecurity theory with real‑world enterprise risks and professional decision‑making. The knowledge gained in Unit 8 will directly support future learning on ethical and security challenges in computing and inform my approach to responsible system design in both academic and professional contexts.
