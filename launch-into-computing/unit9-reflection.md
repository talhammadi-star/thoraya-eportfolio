# Launch into Computing – Unit 9 Reflection
## Computing and Risk

---

## What? (Description)

In Unit 9, I explored how modern enterprises manage computing-related risk and align IT strategy with organisational objectives. The unit introduced the strategic dimension of enterprise IT governance and highlighted how frameworks such as TOGAF, COBIT, and ITIL support executive-level planning, architecture, and governance. The unit also focused on risk as a business concern rather than only a technical issue, linking computing decisions to long-term operational stability and organisational outcomes. As part of this unit, I reviewed the seminar materials that reinforced how technology choices, data, and governance influence risk and decision-making at scale. I also completed the Disaster Recovery Plan Development activity, which required identifying disruptive risks in a cloud-based organisation and producing a step-by-step disaster recovery plan, including tools, methods to protect data integrity, and legal/ethical considerations.

---

## So what? (Interpretation)

This unit strengthened my understanding that enterprise risk management depends on both technology design and governance discipline. Strategic frameworks help organisations make consistent decisions about architecture, investment, and operational control, while disaster recovery planning demonstrates how resilience is engineered, tested, and governed. The focus on recovery objectives and structured response highlights that downtime and data loss are business-impact issues, not only IT issues.

From a professional perspective, these concepts relate strongly to my work in enterprise systems and automation environments, where services must remain available and reliable, and where disruptions can directly affect finance, customer operations, and compliance. Understanding strategic governance and resilience planning helps me evaluate technology choices with a broader view of risk, accountability, and long-term sustainability.

---

## What next? (Action)

Going forward, I will apply a more risk-aware approach when working on enterprise applications and automation workflows, particularly by considering resilience, access governance, and operational recovery early in solution design. I also plan to deepen my understanding of governance frameworks and how they translate into practical controls such as monitoring, incident response, and recovery testing.

Academically, Unit 9 provides a foundation for later units that extend governance and risk into more operational and security-focused perspectives. I will continue documenting my learning in my e-portfolio and linking theory to real-world enterprise constraints, including compliance obligations and continuity requirements.
